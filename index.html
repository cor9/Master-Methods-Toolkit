CHILD ACTOR 101 · MASTER METHODS

The Actor’s Toolkit.

Seven approaches. One goal: truthful, specific, compelling performances. Use these tools to make stronger choices in your scenes, not to perform a technique for its own sake.

There is no single “right” method.

Each technique gives you a different way into the same scene. Start with what your character wants. Then choose the tool that helps you listen, imagine, act, and respond more truthfully.

METHOD 01 / The foundation

STANISLAVSKI

Finding the character’s immediate want and the larger Super Objective that drives their life.

01

CORE TOOLS

Scene objective: what I want right nowSuper Objective: what I want across my storyTactics: how I pursue the want
ASK YOURSELF

What do I need from this person in this scene? What bigger need is driving me?

TRY IT IN YOUR SCENE

Write your scene objective as “I want ___ from ___.” Then name your character’s Super Objective. Notice how the scene serves that larger want.

DIRECTOR'S QUESTION

Can we see you actively pursuing something, not just feeling something?

METHOD 02 / The personal connection

UTA HAGEN

Realism through the Nine Questions, being present in the scene, and clear destinations from the moment before to the final button.

02

CORE TOOLS

The Nine QuestionsDestinations: where I come from and where I goMoment before and button
ASK YOURSELF

Where have I just come from? What is real around me? Where am I going when the scene ends?

TRY IT IN YOUR SCENE

Use the Nine Questions below to make the situation specific. Decide on your moment before, your destination, and the button that ends the scene.

DIRECTOR'S QUESTION

Does your character seem to have a life before the first line and after the last?

METHOD 03 / The other person

MEISNER

Connection with your scene partner or reader: make the relationship real and let their behavior move you to respond.

03

CORE TOOLS

Connection with the reader or partnerLet them move you to moveGet out of your head and really listen
ASK YOURSELF

What did my partner just do that changed me?

TRY IT IN YOUR SCENE

Run the scene once without planning how to say your next line. Watch and listen to your reader; allow what they give you to change your response.

DIRECTOR'S QUESTION

Did you react to the actual person in front of you, or to the version you rehearsed?

METHOD 04 / The sensory life

STRASBERG

Observe precise details so you can recreate them in performance, and use personal experience to relate to the character.

04

CORE TOOLS

Observation and recreation of detailsPersonal experience as a bridge to characterSpecific sensory and emotional details
ASK YOURSELF

What have I noticed in real life that I can truthfully recreate here? What experience helps me understand this character?

TRY IT IN YOUR SCENE

Observe a familiar place or interaction closely. Recreate three precise details in the scene. Connect to a personal experience only if it feels useful and safe.

DIRECTOR'S QUESTION

Which observed detail or personal connection made your performance more specific?

METHOD 05 / The imagined world

STELLA ADLER

Use imagination to build the character’s backstory, memories, and entire world, then transport yourself into a real-feeling place, even on a self-tape or green screen.

05

CORE TOOLS

Imagined backstory and memoriesBuild the full world of the sceneTransform a self-tape or green screen space
ASK YOURSELF

What has my character lived through that is not written on the page? What exists beyond the camera frame?

TRY IT IN YOUR SCENE

Invent one vivid memory and three environmental details: something you see, hear, and physically navigate. Play the scene as though that world surrounds you.

DIRECTOR'S QUESTION

Did your imagined world change where you looked, how you moved, and what mattered?

METHOD 06 / The playable task

PRACTICAL AESTHETICS (MAMET)

Practical Aesthetics: use four questions to turn the scene into a playable action, then make the stakes personal through the AS IF.

06

CORE TOOLS

1. What is happening?2. What do I want?3. What is my universal action?4. What is my AS IF?
ASK YOURSELF

What universal action am I playing, such as “throw me a lifeline” or “get someone on my side”? What is my AS IF?

TRY IT IN YOUR SCENE

Answer all four questions. Choose a universal action you can DO to the other person, then create an AS IF that gives that action personal urgency.

DIRECTOR'S QUESTION

Are you playing a clear action rather than trying to manufacture an emotion?

METHOD 07 / The physical imagination

MICHAEL CHEKHOV

Use body language to access emotion and create a distinctive character through the Psychological Gesture.

07

CORE TOOLS

Body language that reveals characterPsychological GesturePhysical choices that set the performance apart
ASK YOURSELF

What does my character’s deepest need look like as a physical gesture?

TRY IT IN YOUR SCENE

Make one large Psychological Gesture that expresses your character’s inner drive. Repeat it, then reduce it until its energy remains without the big movement.

DIRECTOR'S QUESTION

How did the physical choice change your emotional life and distinguish the character?

WEEK 7 · INTEGRATION LAB

Choose the tool. Play the scene.

Before rehearsal, answer these four questions:

  1. Objective: What do I need from the other person?
  2. Obstacle: What makes it difficult to get?
  3. Shift: What new information changes my approach?
  4. Tool: Which method helps me make this moment more truthful?

Remember: The audience should see a person living through a moment, not an actor demonstrating a method.
